# Problem statement (Round 2, verbatim)

A newspaper ran this headline: "Product injuries surged 18% in 2024, and e-bikes are the deadly new threat. E-bike injuries nearly quadrupled in a year."
A safety regulator has $10M for one national prevention campaign in 2026. A city council is also using the headline to justify banning e-bikes.
Our team are the regulator's analysts. Using the NEISS data:
1. Fact-check the headline. Which parts hold up, which don't, and why?
2. Recommend where the $10M should go: which hazard, which population, which message.
3. Say what we can't conclude from this data, and what extra data would change our answer.

## Our one-sentence business question
Should the regulator spend its one $10M 2026 campaign on e-bikes, as the headline implies, or on the hazard the data says actually costs the most ER visits, admissions and deaths?
